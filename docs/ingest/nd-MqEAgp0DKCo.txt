USS Samuel B. Roberts: the escort that had to be rescued
The United States Navy is not guessing
about convoy escort at Hormuz. It has
done this before. The operation had a
name. The scars are still in the hall
records. Operation Earnest Will. July
24th, 1987 to September 26th, 1988. The
largest American naval convoy operation
since World War II. Kuwaiti oil tankers
were refflged under the stars and
stripes to justify US Navy escort
through the Persian Gulf during the Iran
Iraq war. American warships sailed
alongside tankers. The mission was
simple, get them through. On April 14th,
1988, the frigot USS Samuel B. Roberts
FFG-58 was escorting exactly that kind
of convoy. The ship's radar was active,
weapons were loaded, the crew was
scanning for aircraft and fast boats.
Then a mine detonated beneath the hull.
An Iranian MO8 contact mine, the
predecessor of the SAT FO2 that Iran
manufactures today, ripped a 15 ft hole
through the hull, cracked the keel, and
flooded the engine room before the crew
could react. 10 sailors were injured.
The ship that was supposed to protect
the convoy now had to be saved by the
convoy. 13 months and $89.5 million in
repairs followed. 4 days later, the Navy
launched Operation Praying Mantis, the
largest US surface engagement since
World War II. The response was not more
escorts. The response was direct strikes
against Iranian naval platforms and oil
installations. The lesson from 1988 was
clear. You do not solve the escort
problem by adding more escorts. You
solve it by removing the threat. 38
years later, the physics of this
